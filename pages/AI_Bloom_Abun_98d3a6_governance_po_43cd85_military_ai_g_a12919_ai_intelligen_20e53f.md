## Introduction

Artificial [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) (AI) is reshaping how militaries gather, interpret and act on information. Far from science fiction, AI‑driven intelligence analysis and strategic decision support are now core parts of modern defence planning and operations. These systems ingest vast swathes of sensor data, communications, open‑source feeds and historical records; filter noise from signal; and present commanders and strategic planners with distilled insights and predictions far faster than human analysts could alone. In high‑stakes military settings, this “decision‑advantage” can mean tighter situational awareness, quicker course‑of‑action evaluation and the ability to adapt plans dynamically as conditions change. Yet these same capabilities raise questions about human [control]({{ 'control/' | relative_url }}), reliability, and how such tools reshape the very nature of command and choice in conflict.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.iaps.ai/research/ai-decision-support-systems" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iaps.ai">[Institute for AI Policy and Strategy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iaps.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Institute for AI Policy and StrategyAI Decision Support Systems: A Neglected Source of Military AI Risk — Institute for AI Policy and Str...</span></span></span>

## How AI Transforms Military Intelligence Workflows

AI’s value proposition in intelligence analysis stems from its capacity to fuse and interpret multi‑source information at scale. Traditional analysis workflows depend on humans manually correlating reports from satellites, radar, signals, and human intelligence, a process that can take days or weeks. AI systems — often drawing on machine learning, natural language processing and anomaly detection — can automate summarisation, trend spotting and correlation across thousands of disparate inputs. Platforms now in use or development aim to deliver timely intelligence products that support both operational planning and strategic foresight.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://systematic.com/us/industries/defense/products/sitaware-suite/sitaware-insight/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: systematic.com">[Systematic A]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">systematic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A/SSita Ware Insight: Advanced Decision Support for Commanders</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Systematic A/SSitaWare Insight: Advanced Decision Support for Commanders...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://systematic.com/us/industries/defense/products/sitaware-suite/sitaware-insight/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: systematic.com">[S]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">systematic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A/SSita Ware Insight: Advanced Decision Support for Commanders</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Systematic A/SSitaWare Insight: Advanced Decision Support for Commanders...</span></span></span>

For example, some modern defense analytics platforms prioritise **data fusion**: merging geospatial imagery, sensor feeds, communications intercepts and environmental data into coherent situational pictures. These systems highlight relevant events or anomalies and offer alerts to analysts, reducing the [cognitive]({{ 'broad-access/' | relative_url }}) burden associated with raw data overload. Others target **open‑source intelligence (OSINT)**, converting publicly available media streams into structured insights that deepen understanding of adversary behaviour or emerging socio‑political risks.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://defence-solutions.airbus.com/en/solutions/intelligence/fortion-survin-abi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defence-solutions.airbus.com">[defencesolutions]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defence-solutions.airbus.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">defencesolutions Fortion® Su RVIn ABI | Airbus</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">defencesolutions Fortion® Su RVIn ABI | Airbus</span></span></span>

By rapidly generating and evaluating alternatives, these tools sharpen strategic debates and ensure decision‑makers consider a wider range of possibilities than would be feasible with manual analysis alone.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.iaps.ai/research/ai-decision-support-systems" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iaps.ai">[Institute for AI Policy and Strategy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iaps.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Institute for AI Policy and StrategyAI Decision Support Systems: A Neglected Source of Military AI Risk — Institute for AI Policy and Str...</span></span></span>

## Predictive Decision Support and Real‑Time Assistance

A key shift in military AI is from static analysis to **predictive decision support**. Beyond summarising conditions, systems increasingly aim to anticipate adversary moves, suggest likely next steps and flag risks before they fully materialise. This can accelerate what militaries call the *decision cycle* — from sensing and orientation through choice and action — at speeds that can outpace human cognition. Reducing “cognitive overload” is a common theme among defence leaders who see large volumes of data slowing down planning and response.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/Military-Review/Online-Exclusive/2025-OLE/Modernizing-Military-Decision-Making/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: armyupress.army.mil">[Army University Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">armyupress.army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Army University PressModernizing Military Decision-Making: Integrating AI into Army Planning...</span></span></span>

Operational platforms being developed or fielded integrate AI into command and control, helping ensure that data from dispersed sources flows swiftly up the chain of command. These tools typically maintain [human oversight]({{ 'human-oversight/' | relative_url }}) but provide recommendations and “option sets” that shape how commanders understand evolving situations and make choices accordingly. Research institutions have noted that this fusion of AI with human judgement is central to preserving analytical rigour, transparency and accountability in strategic decision‑making.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://cetas.turing.ac.uk/publications/ai-and-strategic-decision-making" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cetas.turing.ac.uk">[Emerging Tech &amp; Security Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cetas.turing.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Emerging Tech &amp; Security CenterAI and Strategic Decision-Making | Centre for Emerging Technology and SecurityApril 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2024</span></span></span>

Reports indicate the U.S. Department of War and allied forces are deploying large language models inside classified networks to speed up data interpretation and enhance decision support, though they emphasise that final authority remains with human operators. ## Integration Challenges and Limitations

Despite promise, the practical integration of AI into intelligence analysis and decision support is complex and contested. One core challenge is **data quality and bias**: AI tools are only as good as the data they ingest. Incomplete or skewed inputs can produce misleading insights, which, if uncritically accepted, might distort planning. AI systems may also struggle with ambiguous or novel situations lacking clear historical precedent, limiting their reliability in unfamiliar contexts.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03610" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXivThe Use of Artificial Intelligence in Military Intelligence: An Experimental Investigation of Added Value in the Analysis ProcessDec...</span></span></span>

Another challenge is **explainability and trust**. While AI can surface patterns and produce recommendations, commanders need to understand *why* a system made a particular suggestion — especially when strategic stakes are high. Without transparent reasoning, decision‑makers may distrust or over‑rely on systems in ways that erode good judgement. Research emphasises that embedding AI into the military decision loop must preserve meaningful human understanding and oversight, not obscure it behind opaque algorithms.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03610" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXivThe Use of Artificial Intelligence in Military Intelligence: An Experimental Investigation of Added Value in the Analysis ProcessDec...</span></span></span>

There are also **organisational and interoperability hurdles**. Defence forces operate diverse systems across different branches and national partners; integrating AI tools into legacy command and control architectures — while ensuring secure, rapid data sharing — remains a non‑trivial task. Aligning AI outputs with existing planning doctrines and coalition standards requires concerted [governance]({{ 'power/' | relative_url }}) and technical work.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://cetas.turing.ac.uk/publications/ai-and-strategic-decision-making" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cetas.turing.ac.uk">[Emerging Tech &amp; Security Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cetas.turing.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Emerging Tech &amp; Security CenterAI and Strategic Decision-Making | Centre for Emerging Technology and SecurityApril 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2024</span></span></span>

## Implications for Strategy and Command Authority

AI‑enhanced intelligence and decision support do more than speed analysis; they shape how strategic judgement is exercised. Authors analysing military AI point out that these technologies act as “epistemic actors” – influencing which data points are considered salient and framing the decision space presented to commanders. This raises questions about the locus of authority in critical strategic choices. If an AI system highlights particular options as superior, does that subtly steer commanders toward actions they might not otherwise consider? These issues underline the importance of governance frameworks that codify human accountability and ensure AI augments rather than replaces human choice.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://smallwarsjournal.com/2026/01/26/ai-reconfiguration-military-power/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smallwarsjournal.com">[Small Wars Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smallwarsjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ai reconfiguration military power</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Small Wars JournalArtificial Intelligence and a Reconfiguration of Military Power | INSSJanuary 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 26, 2026</span></span></span>
