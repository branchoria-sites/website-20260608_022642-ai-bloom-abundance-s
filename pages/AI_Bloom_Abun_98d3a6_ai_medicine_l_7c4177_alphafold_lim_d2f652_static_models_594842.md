## Introduction

AlphaFold helped transform structural biology by making protein structure prediction dramatically faster and more accessible. For many proteins, researchers can now obtain a useful three-dimensional model in minutes rather than spending months or years solving a structure experimentally. That is a major [scientific]({{ 'discovery/' | relative_url }}) advance and one reason AlphaFold is often cited as evidence that AI could accelerate [discovery]({{ 'discovery/' | relative_url }}) across medicine and biology. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03819-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NatureHighly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFoldby J Jumper · 2021 · Cited by 50175 — The AlphaFold network directly pre...</span></span></span>

But a protein structure is not the same thing as a protein's behaviour. Living proteins are not rigid sculptures. They bend, twist, open, close, bind to other molecules, change shape in response to their environment, and sometimes exist in several different forms at once. A single predicted structure can therefore be highly accurate and still miss some of the most important biology. Understanding that gap is essential for understanding both the promise and the limits of AI-driven protein prediction. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.sciencenews.org/article/alphafold-ai-protein-structure-folding-prediction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencenews.org">[Science News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencenews.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">alphafold ai protein structure folding prediction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A static “structure doesn&#x27;t tell you everything about how a protein works...Read more...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11892350/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAlpha Fold and protein folding: Not dead yet!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The frontier is...by GR Bowman · 2024 · Cited by 31 — AlphaFold is revolutionizing structural biology by providing routine access to hig...</span></span></span>

## Why proteins change shape inside cells

A common mental picture of proteins is that they resemble tiny molecular machines with fixed shapes. In reality, many proteins behave more like moving mechanical systems whose function depends on motion.

Cells are crowded, chemically active environments. Proteins are constantly being jostled by surrounding molecules and water. Their atoms vibrate, their loops flex, and entire domains can swing between different positions. These movements are not merely noise. Often they are central to what the protein actually does. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11892350/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAlpha Fold and protein folding: Not dead yet!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The frontier is...by GR Bowman · 2024 · Cited by 31 — AlphaFold is revolutionizing structural biology by providing routine access to hig...</span></span></span>

Enzymes provide a simple example. Many enzymes alter their shape as they bind a target molecule and carry out a chemical reaction. A structure showing only the "before" state may not reveal how the reaction proceeds. Likewise, transport proteins can shift between open and closed forms as they move substances across membranes. Regulatory proteins may switch conformations when they receive a chemical signal. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.sciencenews.org/article/alphafold-ai-protein-structure-folding-prediction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencenews.org">[Science News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencenews.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">alphafold ai protein structure folding prediction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A static “structure doesn&#x27;t tell you everything about how a protein works...Read more...</span></span></span>

Biologists increasingly describe proteins not as single structures but as **conformational ensembles**: collections of related shapes that exist with different probabilities. Function often depends on movement between these states rather than on any one state alone. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959440X26000357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">static structure prediction achieved by AlphaFold...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/26/4/bbaf340/8202937" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicBeyond static structures: protein dynamic conformations...by X Cui · 2025 · Cited by 34 — This review outlines the fundament...</span></span></span>

This matters because disease can emerge when those motions change. A mutation might not destroy a protein's overall structure yet still alter how easily it shifts between functional states. Looking only at a static snapshot can hide that effect. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11892350/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAlpha Fold and protein folding: Not dead yet!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The frontier is...by GR Bowman · 2024 · Cited by 31 — AlphaFold is revolutionizing structural biology by providing routine access to hig...</span></span></span>

## Where static predictions help and fail

AlphaFold's success comes partly from its ability to infer a highly plausible structure from patterns learned across enormous numbers of experimentally determined proteins. For many research questions, that is exactly what scientists need. A reliable structural model can reveal active sites, suggest protein functions, guide laboratory experiments, and help researchers identify promising drug targets. * **Allosteric regulation.** A molecule binding at one site can trigger structural changes elsewhere in the protein. The problem is not that AlphaFold's predictions are wrong. Often they are remarkably accurate. ## Why drug discovery often needs the missing motions

The importance of protein motion becomes especially clear in drug development.

Many medicines work by fitting into a protein's binding pocket. Yet binding pockets are not always permanently visible. Some appear only briefly when a protein flexes into a particular conformation. Others change size or shape depending on what molecules are nearby. A static model may miss these transient opportunities entirely. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11892350/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAlpha Fold and protein folding: Not dead yet!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The frontier is...by GR Bowman · 2024 · Cited by 31 — AlphaFold is revolutionizing structural biology by providing routine access to hig...</span></span></span>

That difference can alter how well a medicine binds. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959440X26000357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">static structure prediction achieved by AlphaFold...</span></span></span>

This is one reason why structure prediction alone does not automatically translate into rapid cures. Knowing a protein's shape is extremely useful, but turning that knowledge into a safe and effective therapy still requires understanding how the protein behaves in realistic biological conditions. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07487-w" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NatureAccurate structure prediction of biomolecular interactions...by J Abramson · 2024 · Cited by 14654 — Here we describe our AlphaFol...</span></span></span>

## How experiments reveal motion that models miss

The continuing importance of experimental biology becomes clear when researchers try to observe molecular motion directly.

### NMR can watch proteins in solution

Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, usually called NMR, is especially valuable for studying protein dynamics. Unlike techniques that often capture a single structure, NMR can provide information about motions occurring across a range of timescales while proteins remain in solution. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/molecular-biosciences/articles/10.3389/fmolb.2022.906437/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontiersin.org">[Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontiersin.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FrontiersAlphaFold 2 and NMR Spectroscopy: Partners to...by DV Laurents · 2022 · Cited by 107 — This experimental approach provides info...</span></span></span>

Researchers use NMR to investigate flexible regions, transient interactions, folding processes, and disordered proteins that may be difficult to describe with a single structure. This makes it highly complementary to AlphaFold rather than a competitor to it. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/molecular-biosciences/articles/10.3389/fmolb.2022.906437/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontiersin.org">[Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontiersin.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FrontiersAlphaFold 2 and NMR Spectroscopy: Partners to...by DV Laurents · 2022 · Cited by 107 — This experimental approach provides info...</span></span></span>
